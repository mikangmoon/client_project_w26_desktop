Craft a Resume that Tells Your Story

Your resume is often your first impression—an opportunity to highlight your unique strengths, skills, and experiences to potential employers. Whether you’re starting from scratch or looking to polish an existing resume, these resources are here to help you every step of the way.

UMSI students are in high demand, and a clear, well-organized resume will help you stand out among applicants. On this page, you'll find practical tips, templates, and guides—all tailored to the needs of School of Information students and aligned with employer expectations.

Need feedback? You can always meet with a CDO Career Coach to review your materials.

Resume Writing Made Easy: Steps to Success

  1. Download a UMSI-Approved Template: Start with a format that works—no need to design from scratch.
  2. Gather Your Experiences: List jobs, internships, volunteer roles, coursework, and projects most relevant to your goals.
  3. Write Impactful Bullets: Use action verbs and highlight your skills, results, and unique contributions.
  4. Tailor for Each Opportunity: Customize your resume for every job or internship you pursue.
  5. Review and Edit: Proofread for errors, check formatting, and ask for feedback from peers or a career coach.

Optimizing Your Resume for an Applicant Tracking System

What is ATS?

Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) are becoming a common method for large companies to manage high volumes of job applications. According to Columbia University’s Center for Career Education, 90% of Fortune 500 Companies use Applicant Tracking Systems. Although an ATS helps companies initially assess more applicants, the system uses AI technology to electronically scan for specific qualifications, scoring your qualifications based on the position description. If you do not know how to optimize your resume for an ATS, your resume and application could be quickly rejected.

Content
  • Tailor your resume for each position.
  • List as many measurable results as possible in your bullet points.
  • Only abbreviate terms (i.e. B.S. degree) if it is also abbreviated in a job description.
  • Use common names for your section headers (Education, Work Experience, Leadership Experience, Skills).
  • Outside of the job description, look for common language and keywords in current employee LinkedIn proﬁles, particularly those in similar roles.
  • Do not put distinctions (i.e. PhD or CPA) into your header, as ATS systems will pull that into your ﬁrst name/last name box. Instead try to include this information elsewhere.
Formatting
  • Create your resume in a MS Word or Google Docs and then convert to PDF (creating a resume in another program/system and then converting to PDF may not work well in ATS).
  • Use simple formatting. If you’d like to create a more design-based resume, only submit that resume when you know that ATS is not used by the organization.
  • Bullet points (not dashes) are recommended.
  • Avoid headers, footers, tables, templates, borders, lines, symbols other than bullet points, shading, fancy fonts, font colors other than black, tables, columns and boxes.
  • Write out months and years.
  • Don’t wordstuﬀ by adding in hidden or white text to trick the system!
  • Functional resumes without dates might not score as well with newer ATS.

Resume FAQ

  • How long should my resume be?
    For most UMSI students and new grads, one page is best. More experienced professionals may require two pages.
  • Do I need a different resume for each application?
    It’s wise to tailor your resume to the requirements and keywords of each opportunity.
  • Is it okay to include coursework or student projects?
    Absolutely! UMSI coursework and projects are valued by employers, especially if they show relevant skills and impact.
  • How often should I update my resume?
    Update your resume every semester, after major projects, or whenever you gain a new role or responsibility.
